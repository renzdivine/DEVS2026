Create the UI with a clean, intentional, professional design. The result must look like a real website designed by a human UI/UX designer, not an AI-generated template.

## IMPORTANT DESIGN RULES

Follow these rules strictly throughout the entire website.

### 1. DO NOT USE LONG EM DASHES

Do not use the long em dash character "—" anywhere in the UI, headings, descriptions, buttons, cards, navigation, or other text.

Use commas, periods, colons, parentheses, or short hyphens where appropriate.

For example:

BAD:
"Creative developer — building modern digital experiences"

GOOD:
"Creative developer building modern digital experiences"

Do not automatically insert em dashes into marketing copy.

### 2. DO NOT CREATE THREE IDENTICAL CARDS IN A ROW

Avoid the common AI-generated layout where three nearly identical cards are placed side by side.

Do not create:

Card 1 | Card 2 | Card 3

when all three cards have the same visual structure, same size, same icon treatment, same amount of text, and only slightly different content.

Instead, create visual variety and hierarchy.

If cards are necessary, vary their size, content structure, importance, or placement.

For example:

• One large featured card
• Two smaller supporting cards
• A project preview with a different layout
• A text-based information section
• A horizontal list
• A timeline
• A visual project showcase

The layout should feel intentionally designed rather than generated from a repeated component.

### 3. NEVER INVENT NUMBERS

Do not create fake statistics, fake metrics, fake counters, fake achievements, fake client numbers, fake project counts, fake percentages, fake years of experience, fake ratings, fake revenue, fake users, or fake performance numbers.

Do not add things such as:

"50+ Projects"
"99% Satisfaction"
"120+ Clients"
"5 Years Experience"
"98% Success Rate"
"10K Users"
"24/7 Support"

unless these exact numbers are provided by me.

If real numbers are not available, simply do not display a number.

Use descriptive content instead.

BAD:
"50+ Projects Completed"

GOOD:
"Selected Projects"

BAD:
"98% Client Satisfaction"

GOOD:
"Client-focused development"

Do not use random numbers as visual decoration.

### 4. NO FAKE CONTENT

Do not invent people, clients, companies, testimonials, reviews, projects, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, social media accounts, awards, statistics, job titles, or case studies.

Only use information that I provide.

If information is missing, leave the section out or use a neutral placeholder that clearly indicates missing content.

Do not create fake names such as:

"Alex Morgan"
"John Smith"
"Sarah Williams"

unless I explicitly provide them.

Do not create fake testimonials.

Do not create fake project titles just to fill empty space.

Do not create fake GitHub repository counts.

Do not create fake social media followers.

Do not create fake company logos.

### 5. NO GREEN AND BLUE STATUS DOTS

Do not use the common AI-generated green dot and blue dot UI elements.

Do not add decorative status indicators such as:

🟢 Available
🔵 Online
🟢 Open to Work
🔵 Active

Do not place green or blue circular dots beside names, buttons, headings, profile pictures, or navigation elements.

Do not create floating green and blue dot decorations simply because the interface looks empty.

If status information is actually necessary, represent it using text, a subtle label, an icon, or another appropriate UI element.

Do not use colored dots as decoration.

### 6. AVOID GENERIC AI HERO SECTIONS

Do not create a generic SaaS-style hero section.

Avoid the typical structure:

"HELLO, I'M"

"Alex Morgan"

"Web Designer & Digital Creative"

"I create modern digital experiences..."

[Start a Project] [Explore Work]

followed by random floating cards and decorative circles.

The hero should be based on the actual information I provide.

Do not invent a person, identity, profession, biography, statistics, or claims.

Use strong typography, meaningful spacing, real content, and a clear visual hierarchy.

### 7. DO NOT OVERUSE ROUNDED CARDS

Avoid putting every piece of information inside a rounded rectangle.

Not everything needs to be a card.

Do not turn:

headings,
paragraphs,
navigation,
skills,
projects,
statistics,
buttons,
social links,
and descriptions

into separate floating cards.

Use normal page sections, typography, whitespace, dividers, grids, images, and editorial layouts.

Cards should only be used when they provide an actual structural purpose.

### 8. AVOID EXCESSIVE GLASSMORPHISM

Do not automatically use:

glassmorphism,
blurred backgrounds,
transparent cards,
glowing borders,
gradient blobs,
neon shadows,
floating translucent panels,
excessive backdrop blur.

Do not use these effects simply to make the website appear "modern."

If glass or blur is used, it should have a clear design purpose and should be used sparingly.

### 9. NO RANDOM GRADIENT BLOBS

Do not add random gradient circles, blobs, glowing spheres, abstract shapes, or floating objects in the background.

Do not fill empty space with meaningless decorative elements.

Whitespace is acceptable.

A clean empty area is better than a meaningless gradient shape.

### 10. NO RANDOM 3D OBJECTS

Do not add random 3D models, floating phones, laptops, cubes, spheres, keyboards, computer screens, or abstract objects unless they are directly relevant to the actual content.

Do not use 3D elements just because AI design systems often use them.

### 11. AVOID EXCESSIVE ICONS

Do not place an icon beside every heading, card, button, or piece of text.

Avoid repetitive icon patterns such as:

[Icon] Web Development
[Icon] Mobile Development
[Icon] UI Design

[Icon] PHP
[Icon] React
[Icon] Next.js

Use icons only when they improve understanding.

Typography and layout should carry most of the visual hierarchy.

### 12. DO NOT USE GENERIC STOCK-STYLE COPY

Avoid generic phrases such as:

"Crafting digital experiences"
"Building the future"
"Where creativity meets technology"
"Empowering businesses through innovation"
"Transforming ideas into reality"
"Taking your brand to the next level"
"Your vision, our expertise"
"Digital solutions that make a difference"

unless they are specifically part of the provided content.

Use concise, specific language based on the actual website and its purpose.

### 13. USE REALISTIC CONTENT HIERARCHY

The design should prioritize actual content.

Use a clear hierarchy:

Primary information
Secondary information
Supporting information
Actions

Do not make every section visually loud.

Not every heading needs to be huge.

Not every button needs to be filled.

Not every section needs animation.

### 14. USE VARIED LAYOUTS

The website should not feel like:

Hero
Three cards
Three cards
Three cards
Testimonials
Three cards
CTA

Instead, create a natural editorial flow.

Possible layouts include:

• Large typography section
• Split layout
• Featured project
• Asymmetric project grid
• Horizontal project list
• Image and text combination
• Full-width project preview
• Skills arranged naturally
• Timeline
• Profile section
• Contact section
• Simple footer

Use different layouts where appropriate.

Do not repeat the same component structure throughout the entire page.

### 15. PROJECTS SHOULD FEEL REAL

Project sections should prioritize the actual project.

Show:

Project name
Project description
Technology used
Role or contribution when provided
Relevant image or preview
Link when available

Do not invent project statistics.

Do not add fake "12K views", "98% performance", "Award Winner", or similar labels.

A project can be visually strong without fake metrics.

### 16. DO NOT CREATE FAKE TESTIMONIALS

If testimonials are not provided, do not generate them.

Do not create fake client quotes.

Do not create fake profile photos.

Do not create fake company names.

If there are no testimonials, remove the testimonial section entirely.

### 17. DO NOT CREATE FAKE SOCIAL PROOF

Avoid:

★★★★★
"Trusted by 100+ clients"
"Used by leading companies"
"5-star rating"
"Featured in..."
"10,000+ users"

unless the information is actually provided.

Do not use social proof simply to make the website look more professional.

### 18. NAVIGATION SHOULD BE SIMPLE

Do not overload the navigation.

Use only the pages and links that actually exist.

Do not create navigation links for nonexistent pages.

Do not create links such as:

/contact
/projects
/about
/services

unless those pages actually exist.

Do not generate localhost URLs or fake routes.

Do not create buttons that lead nowhere.

Every interactive element should have a real destination or an intentional interaction.

### 19. NO FAKE BUTTONS

Do not add buttons just to fill space.

Every button must have a purpose.

Avoid excessive CTAs such as:

Start a Project
Let's Talk
View Work
Explore More
Learn More
Get Started
Contact Me

all appearing within the same section.

Use only the actions that are actually relevant.

### 20. TYPOGRAPHY SHOULD FEEL HUMAN-DESIGNED

Use a limited typography system.

Avoid using huge text everywhere.

Avoid making every heading uppercase.

Avoid excessive letter spacing.

Avoid randomly changing font weights.

Use typography to create hierarchy rather than decorative effects.

Headings should be readable and intentional.

Body text should have comfortable line height and reasonable width.

### 21. SPACING SHOULD BE CONSISTENT

Use a consistent spacing system.

Avoid sections that are:

too cramped,
too empty,
randomly spaced,
or vertically stretched just to fill the screen.

Do not add unnecessary empty cards or containers to create spacing.

Use actual padding, margin, grid gaps, and whitespace.

### 22. RESPONSIVE DESIGN

The design must work naturally on:

Desktop
Laptop
Tablet
Mobile

Do not simply shrink the desktop layout.

On mobile:

• Stack content logically
• Maintain readable typography
• Avoid horizontal overflow
• Make buttons accessible
• Keep navigation usable
• Preserve visual hierarchy
• Avoid oversized decorative elements

### 23. DARK AND LIGHT MODE

If both dark mode and light mode are included, do not simply invert every color.

Design each mode intentionally.

Maintain:

Readable contrast
Clear hierarchy
Consistent borders
Consistent surfaces
Accessible buttons
Natural imagery

Avoid excessive neon colors in dark mode.

Avoid pure white backgrounds with random blue accents in light mode unless they fit the actual visual identity.

### 24. COLORS

Use a controlled color palette.

Do not automatically use:

blue + purple gradients,
green + blue status indicators,
neon purple,
neon cyan,
random orange accents,
rainbow gradients.

Choose colors based on the actual brand or design direction.

If no brand colors are provided, use a restrained neutral palette with one intentional accent color.

### 25. ANIMATIONS

Use animation only when it improves the experience.

Avoid:

constant floating animations,
random bouncing elements,
excessive parallax,
text continuously moving,
cards endlessly floating,
random glowing effects.

Animations should be subtle and purposeful.

Use transitions for:

hover states,
navigation,
image reveals,
section transitions,
interactive elements.

Do not animate everything.

### 26. IMAGES

Use images only when they contribute to the content.

Do not insert random stock images to fill empty areas.

Do not invent photographs of people.

Do not add fake team members.

If an image is unavailable, create an intentional empty state or placeholder instead of inventing content.

### 27. HUMAN DESIGN PRINCIPLE

The final result should look like someone intentionally designed the interface around actual content.

It should NOT look like an AI took a generic portfolio template and filled it with random content.

Avoid predictable AI patterns.

Avoid repetitive cards.

Avoid excessive rounded rectangles.

Avoid fake numbers.

Avoid fake testimonials.

Avoid fake people.

Avoid fake logos.

Avoid random icons.

Avoid decorative dots.

Avoid gradient blobs.

Avoid unnecessary glassmorphism.

Avoid excessive shadows.

Avoid meaningless animations.

Avoid excessive text.

Avoid generic marketing language.

Avoid visual clutter.

### 28. CONTENT ACCURACY

Use only the information supplied in the project requirements.

If a piece of information is unknown, do not guess.

If a number is unknown, do not invent one.

If a person is unknown, do not invent one.

If a project is unknown, do not invent one.

If a social link is unknown, do not create one.

If a page does not exist, do not link to it.

If there are only two real projects, show two projects.

Do not create a third project just to make a three-column grid.

If there are four real projects, show four.

The number of UI elements must match the actual available content.

### 29. PRIORITIZE CONTENT OVER TEMPLATE PATTERNS

Do not force the content into a predefined AI layout.

The layout must adapt to the content.

For example, if there are two projects, use a two-project layout.

If there is one featured project, make it prominent.

If there are five skills, display five skills naturally.

If there are no testimonials, remove the testimonial section.

If there are no statistics, do not create a statistics section.

If there are only three team members, show those three team members without inventing additional people.

The final UI should feel minimal, professional, intentional, realistic, and human-designed.

The goal is not to make the interface look "fancy."

The goal is to make it look like a real designer carefully designed a real product using real content.
